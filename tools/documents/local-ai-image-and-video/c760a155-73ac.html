Getting Started with Local AI Image & Video Generation

2026-10-19 ·

Before you start

Your GPU's VRAM decides which models you can load at all, so check it first and pick models from your tier below. Speed matters less than whether the model fits.

Check your VRAM. On Windows, open Task Manager (Ctrl+Shift+Esc) > Performance > GPU and read Dedicated GPU memory. Ignore "Shared GPU memory"; that is borrowed system RAM, not real VRAM. You can also type nvidia-smi in Command Prompt.

Check the rest of the PC.

  • System RAM: 16 GB minimum, 32 GB recommended, because ComfyUI offloads parts of big models into system RAM.

  • Disk space: model files are large, often 5–20 GB each. Put ComfyUI on an SSD with 100 GB or more free.

  • GPU brand: these steps assume NVIDIA. AMD works on Windows now but is newer and less reliable.

What each VRAM tier can do (video tiers, image tiers):

Set expectations. Images take seconds to a minute or two. Video takes minutes per short clip, even on good cards, so plan to queue a render and walk away.

Install ComfyUI

Use Comfy Desktop, the official installer. It sets up ComfyUI, Python and every dependency for you, so there is nothing to configure by hand. It needs Windows 10 or later, about 4.85 GB per install, and 8 GB of RAM (16 GB recommended).

  1. Update your NVIDIA driver through the NVIDIA app before you start.

  2. Download and run the installer from comfy.org/download. Launch Comfy Desktop from the Start Menu when it finishes.

  3. Choose Local when the setup asks "How do you want to run Comfy?" The Cloud option runs on Comfy's servers instead of your GPU.

  4. Tick the EULA box. It is required. The anonymous usage data and beta features boxes are ticked by default; untick them if you prefer.

  5. Leave "Express Install" unticked the first time. The wizard then lets you pick the install location; choose a drive with plenty of free space. If you do use Express Install, check that the detected GPU it names is your NVIDIA card.

  6. Launch your instance from the dashboard once the install finishes. ComfyUI opens as a canvas of connected boxes (nodes).

Good to know:

  • Downloaded models live in a shared library at %LOCALAPPDATA%\Comfy-Desktop\ComfyUI-Shared, along with your outputs.

  • When an update is available, a Desktop Update Ready button appears at the top of the window.

  • Uninstalling the app does not delete your models or outputs; those folders must be removed by hand (details).

Your first image

Start with Z-Image Turbo from the built-in templates. It gives Flux-level photorealism in 8 steps and runs on 8 GB of VRAM in its fp8 version (guide). Templates come pre-wired, so you skip building a workflow by hand.

  1. Open the template browser: Workflow > Browse Workflow Templates.

  2. Pick "Z-Image-Turbo Text to Image." It sits in the getting-started group.

  3. Download the missing models when ComfyUI prompts you. Z-Image needs three files: the diffusion model, the text encoder (qwen_3_4b, about 7 GB) and the VAE (ae.safetensors).

  4. Under 16 GB of VRAM, swap in the fp8 model. Download z_image_turbo_fp8_e4m3fn.safetensors (link) into models/diffusion_models, then select it in the diffusion model loader box.

  5. Type a prompt and click Run. The image appears on the canvas and saves to your output folder.

If you get a gray, noisy image with no error, one of the three files is missing from its loader box.

Image basics: settings and prompts

Keep the template's defaults at first and change one setting at a time. The settings live in the KSampler box. The biggest beginner mistake is copying old Stable Diffusion values onto newer "distilled" models, which are built for few steps and low CFG.

SettingWhat it doesStarting point
SeedThe random starting noise. Same seed and settings give the same image.Randomize while exploring; fix it once you like a result, then tweak the prompt
StepsHow many refinement passes the model makesDistilled models: 4–8 (Z-Image: 8). SDXL and Flux.1 Dev: 20–40
CFGHow strictly the model follows the promptDistilled models: 1–2 (Z-Image: 1.5–2.0). SDXL: 5–8
Sampler / schedulerThe math used for each stepLeave the template's choice
ResolutionOutput size in pixelsThe model's native size, about 1024×1024. Upscale afterward rather than generating at 2K

On Z-Image, raising CFG to 4 or more makes images worse, not more accurate (source). If a prompt isn't landing, reword it or try a new seed.

Prompting modern models: write plain sentences, not keyword lists. Describe the subject, the setting, the lighting ("soft window light") and the framing ("medium close-up, 85mm lens, shallow depth of field"). Skip filler like "masterpiece, best quality, 8k"; newer models ignore it. SDXL is the exception: it responds better to comma-separated tags.

Your first video

Start with Wan 2.2, the open-source quality leader with the best ComfyUI support, and switch to LTX-2.3 when you want speed or sound (model guide). Wan 2.2 is the version to download: the newer Wan 2.5 and 2.6 are cloud-only.

  1. Open the template browser and go to the video workflows. Pick a Wan 2.2 workflow that matches your VRAM tier from the table below.

  2. Start with image-to-video. Generate a still with Z-Image, then animate it. It is far easier to control than describing a whole scene from text.

  3. Keep the first run small: about 512 pixels wide and 49 frames. Raise resolution one step at a time after a run succeeds.

  4. Click Run and walk away. Check back in a few minutes.

Expect your card to be slower than these benchmarks. An RTX 3090 takes about 40–50% longer than a 4090, and older mid-range cards slower still.

Speed tip: add the community Lightning LoRA to Wan 2.2. It cuts generation to 4 steps; one test went from a 40–50 minute render to 1–3 minutes, with lower peak VRAM (source).

Licenses: Wan 2.2 is Apache 2.0, free for any use. LTX-2.3 uses Lightricks' community license, free under $10M in revenue.

Making models fit your VRAM

Most "it won't run" problems are solved by downloading a smaller version of the same model, not by changing settings. Under 16 GB of VRAM, never download the full BF16/FP16 file "to be safe"; it will not load, and it wastes 20–40 GB of disk (source).

The file versions you will see:

  • BF16 / FP16: full precision. Best quality, largest file, most VRAM.

  • FP8: half the size with quality close to full. Usually the fastest option that fits.

  • GGUF (Q8 down to Q3): compressed further; lower numbers are smaller and lose more quality. Lowest VRAM floor, but needs the ComfyUI-GGUF loader node and leans on system RAM (32 GB recommended).

  • Distilled: a version trained to finish in far fewer steps. Faster, slightly less detail.

When you hit "CUDA out of memory":

  1. Confirm you loaded the FP8 or GGUF version, not the full one.

  2. Lower the resolution or frame count. This is usually the real fix.

  3. In ComfyUI, open Settings > Server config and set VRAM management mode to lowvram.

  4. Close other GPU-heavy apps, including games and browsers with hardware acceleration, then retry.

When it runs but crawls: ComfyUI has quietly moved part of the model into system RAM because VRAM is full. Watch GPU usage in Task Manager; near 0% means it is offloading. Drop to a smaller version of the model.

Finding models and workflows safely

Get models from the template browser, Hugging Face or Civitai, only as .safetensors files, and treat custom nodes as software you are installing on your PC.

  • Hugging Face is where most models are officially published. The Comfy-Org repositories repackage files so they drop straight into ComfyUI.

  • Civitai hosts community fine-tunes and LoRAs. Check that each file's listed base model matches yours; for example, SDXL LoRAs don't work on Z-Image (source). It also hosts a lot of adult content, so set its content filters in your account settings.

  • Prefer .safetensors. That format holds only model weights. Older .ckpt, .pt and .pth files can contain code that runs when loaded.

  • Be careful with custom nodes. They are Python code with full access to your PC. In 2024, a node called ComfyUI_LLMVISION stole users' browser passwords and credit card details (404 Media). Install only widely used, maintained nodes through the Manager, and be wary of shared workflows that require obscure ones.

  • Keep ComfyUI on your own machine. Don't open it to the internet or your wider network.

Tip: images made in ComfyUI carry their workflow inside the PNG. Drag one onto the canvas to load the exact setup that made it.

Next steps, and when to upgrade

Once basic images and clips work, these add the most control. Each has a built-in template or a well-known workflow.

  • LoRAs: small add-on files that steer a model toward a style or character. Use one trained on your base model, at strength 0.6–1.0; drop each to 0.3–0.5 when stacking several (source).

  • Image editing: FLUX.2 Klein and Qwen-Image-Edit templates change an existing image from a text instruction.

  • ControlNet: guides composition using a pose, depth map or outline taken from a reference image.

  • Upscaling: generate at native size, then upscale and run a second pass at about 0.3 denoise to add detail without changing the image.

  • Video polish: upscale clips with RealESRGAN and smooth motion with RIFE frame interpolation (source).

Upgrade when your card is the bottleneck, not before. The signs are constant out-of-memory errors at the settings you want, or waits long enough that you stop experimenting. For video, 16 GB is the minimum worth buying, and 24 GB runs every current model (source).

Rent before you buy. RunPod lists an RTX 4090 at about $0.34–0.69 an hour with one-click ComfyUI templates (source, July 2026). A few hours shows you what a 24 GB card would change. Stop the pod when you finish, since billing runs until you do.
